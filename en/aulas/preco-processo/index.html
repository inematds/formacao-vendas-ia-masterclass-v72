Price, objections and the engine of the process

The last lesson closes the cycle: what to say when they ask for the price too early, why the people who haggle the most tend to be the worst clients, how the objection changes with the stage, and which numbers in your process turn luck into method. And the work that starts after the signature: the ongoing advisory.

01 · The engine of the process

Four steps and what comes after. Tap each stage.

A five-column spreadsheet does the job. Start measuring, even if it's on paper.

02 · Price without becoming a commodity

A commodity is being chosen on price alone. The price table lives in courses 2 and 4; here the focus is the conversation.

1. "How much does it cost?" early

At the end of the conversation, it is a buying signal: answer directly. In the first two minutes, it is a request to turn you into a line in a comparison.

Counter-question: "so I can answer you properly, is price what will decide this, or is something else weighing on it?".

Speak lightlyI could tell you a million right now and we'd wrap up here, but you'd see how pointless that is, because I still don't know what's going on over there. Let me understand the problem first.

2. The honest range

Give a wide range and say what makes the number move: scope (how many processes are included), integration (how many systems it connects to) and ongoing advisory (how long I stay with you).

"Some clients spend US$ 40 and some spend US$ 40 thousand" is true and brings the conversation back to the problem (illustrative values: adapt to your market).

3. Who haggles too much

Legitimate negotiation: "that's above what I can do right now, can we start with a smaller part?". Reduce scope, not the price for the same work.

If you give in on price, give in on scope too. A pure discount teaches them your number was inflated.

4. Objection by stage

Top: fear of getting it wrong, teach. Middle: comparison, set yourself apart by your understanding and by what you don't do. Bottom: timing, reduce scope or set a future start with a date.

The question that separates them: "Expensive compared to the budget, or to what you think this will return?". Basic objections: course 3.

03 · After signing, and your copilot

Where monthly advisory begins and where AI helps.

1. The first week

Agree on a start date, send what was agreed and make a small, visible delivery right at the beginning. A new client is an insecure client.

"Got it, I'll look at it today and get back to you by tomorrow" settles a good part of the anxiety.

2. Monthly advisory

Keeping a client takes delivery and communication; winning one from scratch takes prospecting. The second is always more expensive.

After the 90-day mentorship, monthly advisory keeps the metric reviewed and the trust alive.

3. Automate the notice, never the delivery

An automatic reply like "got it, I'll get back to you by tomorrow" is useful. A delivery done on autopilot, with no one looking at it, is the start of a lost client.

4. Your sales copilot

It helps a lot with: pre-conversation research, scripts by sector, simulating a difficult client, and analyzing your notes and your pipeline.

It doesn't replace you: noticing a change in tone, waiting three more seconds in silence, admitting a limit of your service in front of the client.

04 · Template to copy

Find the bottleneck and leave with a single experiment.

Prompt · audit of your sales process
You are my sales process auditor. Be specific and avoid generic advice.
MY NUMBERS FROM LAST MONTH
- Conversations started: <n>
- Became a real diagnosis: <n>
- Became a proposal presented live: <n>
- Closed: <n>
- Average ticket: <value>
HOW I WORK TODAY
- Opening: <what I do in the first minutes>
- Root questions I usually ask: <list>
- How I talk about price: <describe>
- How I end conversations: <describe>
Deliver:
1. Where the BIGGEST bottleneck is, with the math that supports that conclusion.
2. The three most likely causes of that bottleneck, tied to what I described above.
3. ONE experiment for the next 2 weeks: what to change, at which stage,
   and which number has to move to say it worked.
4. What I should NOT touch right now, so I don't confuse the results.
